What you get

Alerts while the food is still good.

01

Out-of-range alerts

Each unit gets its own safe high and low, pre-set for cooler or freezer. A cracked door, a dying compressor, an iced coil — you hear about it in minutes, not at the morning open.

02

A silent probe is an alarm

If a probe loses power or Wi-Fi, that silence itself raises an alert. A dark freezer never quietly passes as "fine".

03

The log, automatically

Every reading recorded and exportable to a spreadsheet whenever you want it — less clipboard time for your staff, and a record you actually have when someone asks.

04

Your data stays yours

Readings live on your machine, in your building. No account, no cloud service, no subscription, and no vendor who can switch it off or put the price up.

No surprises

What this is, and what it isn't.

You are letting a stranger put equipment in your kitchen, so here is the part most vendors leave for the small print.

What we need from you

  • A spot for the hub computer on your Wi-Fi, and access to place the probes.
  • About 30 minutes for setup, and a name and number for whoever should get alerts.
  • Your honest feedback at the end — did it catch anything, was it easy, what would you actually pay for it.

What it is not

  • It is an operational monitoring and loss-prevention tool. It is not a certified, NIST-traceable or health-code compliance instrument, and it does not replace your required manual checks or official records.
  • Everything runs on power. A full outage stops monitoring — though your coolers are off then too. We can put the hub on a small battery backup if that matters.
  • Alerts that leave the building ride your internet. If the WAN drops, the on-screen alarm still shows but email pauses until it is back.

Why you can trust the numbers

Built and tested in the open.

This isn't a rebadged import. The hardware, the firmware and the software are designed here, the source is public, and the field testing is published — including what it got wrong. Three probes logged 430,022 readings across two days; the same run surfaced a timestamp bug, which is written up alongside the good numbers rather than quietly dropped.
