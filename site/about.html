Datum Labs · About

I'm Thomas Allen. I build embedded products end to end.

Founder of Datum Laboratories — a one-person shop where the person who talks to you is the one who designs the board, writes the firmware, and builds the software around it. I like taking an idea all the way to a working, reliable, manufacturable thing. Setpoint is one I took the whole way.

See what I offer → Setpoint

The short version: I ship.

Plenty of people can list "ESP32" and "Python" on a page. Fewer can point to a product they designed, built, and put in customers' hands — hardware, firmware, and software, all of it. Setpoint is a no-cloud Wi-Fi temperature logger I took from concept to a shipping DIY kit: I designed the ESP32-C3 hardware and carrier, wrote the firmware (offline buffering, deep-sleep battery modes, browser-based flashing), built the self-hosted dashboard and REST/MQTT/Prometheus back end, and wrote the documentation so a buyer is never locked in.

Then I field-tested it — because a spec sheet isn't proof. One unit logged 160,916 readings over 46 continuous hours on a single charge with zero dropouts, and the write-up is honest about exactly what that does and doesn't establish. That's how I work: build it, test it, and tell you the truth about the result.

Product

Setpoint

Shipping now

The problem: Off-the-shelf temperature loggers are cloud-locked, subscription-bound, or too dumb to trust with a freezer full of food or a server rack.

What I built: A self-contained Wi-Fi probe on an ESP32-C3 + DS18B20 — custom carrier board, low-power firmware with an on-device offline buffer and deep-sleep modes, a browser flasher (no toolchain), and a self-hosted Python dashboard with alerts, CSV/Excel export, and MQTT/Prometheus/Home-Assistant integrations. No cloud, no account.

Outcome: Shipping as a DIY kit on Tindie with a small-batch Rev-2 in production, and field-tested over multi-day runs. See Setpoint →

Embedded vision

Wearable vision system

Uncommon build

The goal: A head-mounted, real-time camera-vision headset with night vision — the kind of integration very few people have actually built end to end.

What I built: An NVIDIA Jetson embedded-Linux pipeline driving live camera vision to a head-mounted display — low-light/night-vision imaging, real-time video, power management for a wearable, and custom + 3D-printed mechanical housing to carry it all.

Outcome: A working, self-contained rig that pulls embedded Linux, optics, real-time vision, power, and mechanical design into one wearable. Code on GitHub →

Local AI

AI inventory & OCR system

Runs offline

The problem: Cataloging items by hand is slow and error-prone, and most "AI" tools want to send your data to someone else's cloud.

What I built: A local-AI pipeline — OCR and vision models running on hardware you own — that reads, extracts, and organizes items automatically, with the automation glue to make it a hands-off workflow. Nothing leaves the machine.

Outcome: A tedious manual task turned into an automated, private pipeline — the same "keep it local, keep it yours" philosophy behind Setpoint.

Also in the mix: ESP32 firmware and sensor work, small networking and automation utilities, test fixtures, and open-source code on GitHub.
